  Summary
  Three things need you personally: choosing and paying the illustrator, engaging and
  paying the attorney, and approving the public release. Claude can't sign contracts, make
  payments, or put a children's app live on your behalf. Everything up to your signature is
  prepared below, and both outreach emails are drafted in chat.

  Order of work

     1. This week: send the attorney inquiry first. Legal review is the longest pole and gates
        the release.
     2. This week: send the illustration brief to two or three agencies and ask for artist
        suggestions and quotes.
     3. Within 3 weeks: sign both engagements; the attorney's first answers unlock the
        accounts-and-consent build.
     4. December to February: illustration production runs alongside the backend build and
        the G2 and G3 gates.
     5. Spring 2027: pilot with real families (G5), then public release once every item in
        section 5 is checked.

  Budget reality check

         The Phase 1 lean budget assumed $8–25k for art and $5–15k for legal.
         Top-tier names cost more. A senior partner at a leading national firm often bills well
         above $1,000 an hour, and an agency-represented children's illustrator for a full game
         world can run into the tens of thousands. These are rough market impressions, not
         quotes.
         The way to get top quality inside the budget is a fixed-fee, tightly scoped engagement
         for each, as sections 2 and 4 describe.


  Illustration: who to approach

  many of them. The agent matches the artist, negotiates rights and manages the schedule,
  which matters for a first-time commissioner.

  What "best for ages 7–10" means for this product

         Bold, readable shapes that work small on a tablet, not delicate picture-book texture.
         A look that feels grown-up enough for a 9- or 10-year-old; a preschool style will lose
         them.
         Experience with characters drawn many times (model sheets, expressions), not single
         illustrations.
         Comfort with flat vector or layered files that engineers can animate.
         A portfolio showing children of many backgrounds, drawn without stereotypes.

  Illustration commissioning brief
  The brief asks the artist to turn the approved drafts (Pip, the night-lantern palette,
  Number Kingdom) into a finished, animatable art set in two stages, so you can stop after
  stage 1 if the style isn't right.

  Project: Tenbright, a math-learning app for ages 7–10 built around place value ("ten little
  lights make one bright idea"). Audience: US families and homeschoolers. Platforms: tablet
  first, then phone and web.

  Stage 1: style and characters (fixed fee; about 3–4 weeks)

         Pip: final design, turnaround (front, side, back, three-quarter), and 8 expressions:
         happy, curious, thinking, "a clue!", celebrating, sleepy, surprised, encouraging.
         Supporting cast: Marta the Mapkeeper, the three Rounders, Captain Carry and
         Lieutenant Lend.
         Two style frames: the Kingdom map, and one practice screen with beads and lanterns.
         Approval point: you sign off before stage 2 starts.

  Stage 2: the world and the interface (fixed fee; about 6–8 weeks)

         Kingdom map with five regions, plus a background for each region.
         Game cover and app-store key art.
         Manipulatives as separate layers: beads, lanterns, towers, base-ten blocks, soroban,
         number line markers, ten-frames.
         Icon set (about 30 icons: hint, listen, map, check, settings, lock and others).
         Certificate design, app icon, and a 6-frame animation guide for Pip (idle, talk,
         celebrate).

  Must-haves

         Original work only: no generative AI imagery, no references to existing franchises.
         Readable at small sizes; shapes that hold up in both light and dark backgrounds.
         Children of many backgrounds, no stereotypes; reviewed together before final.
         Delivery as layered source files (vector or layered PSD) plus exports at 1×, 2× and 3×.

  Lower-cost routes to the same expertise

         Ask the chosen firm for a fixed-fee COPPA review led by the partner and staffed by
         an associate.
         An FTC-approved Safe Harbor program (kidSAFE, PRIVO) reviews your practices as
         part of membership. It's not a substitute for a lawyer, but it reduces what the lawyer
         must do.

  I could not confirm each lawyer's current availability or rates; confirm both on the first call.


  Attorney engagement scope
  Ask for one fixed-fee engagement that ends with written sign-off for the pilot and a
  punch list for public launch. "Make all necessary corrections" works best when the
  attorney marks up and you or Claude implement the changes, so legal hours go to
  judgment, not editing.

  What they review

     1. The Accounts, Consent and Sync Plan and its 8 questions.
     2. The live MVP, end to end, as a parent and as a child.
     3. Drafts we will prepare: the direct notice to parents, the online privacy policy, terms of
         service with clear subscription cancellation, and the data retention policy.
     4. The written information security program.
     5. The illustration contract's rights clauses.
     6. The marketing claims plan: no learning-outcome or "brain" claims without evidence.
     7. Trademark clearance for "Tenbright" (Classes 9 and 41), or a referral for it.

  What we want back

         A redline of every document, and a list of required changes to the app.
         Written answers to the 8 questions.
         A go or no-go for a consented pilot, and the conditions for public launch.

  How corrections get made: I can implement the app and document changes from their
  redlines within the same day. Anything they mark as needing their own drafting stays with
  them.
