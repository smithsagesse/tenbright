  Tenbright Art Commission Brief
    Nov 3, 2026        ·   ​@Smith




  Summary
  Tenbright needs one illustrator, or one small studio, to create a single visual world for
  children aged 7–10: Pip the guide, five map regions, the app icon, key art, and illustrated
  skins for the math models. The app's colors, type and layout are already set by the
  Tenbright design system; the artist adds character and place on top of it.

  No one illustrator is objectively "the best". The best fit is one who has shipped children's
  educational or game art at this age band, can draw quantities exactly, and delivers
  layered, app-ready files. That points to agencies that specialise in children's and
  educational illustration rather than open freelance marketplaces.

  Recommended route

     1. Send the brief in section 7 to the three agencies in section 2 on the same day and ask
        each for 3–5 portfolio matches with availability and a quote.
     2. Pay 2–3 finalists a fixed fee for a test sheet: Pip in three expressions plus one region
        thumbnail.
     3. Test the sheets with 5–8 children aged 7–10 during the G3 sessions; pick the artist
        children recognise and describe positively and who scores best against the rules in
        section 3.
     4. Contract on the terms in section 5 before any production work.

  I can't contact, hire or pay anyone. This brief, the shortlist and the email are ready for you
  to send.


  Shortlist

  Art direction for ages 7–10
  Children at 7–10 reject art that looks "babyish" but still respond to warmth and humour.
  The look should sit between a picture book and a chapter-book or game cover: confident
  shapes, real-world texture, a sense of adventure.

  Style

         Warm, hand-made feel with clean, readable silhouettes; no photorealism, no generic
         stock-vector look.
         Palette built on the design system: cream background, navy, amber accent, river blue
         and meadow green. The artist may add tints and shadows but not new brand colors.
         Diverse children shown in everyday settings, never as stereotypes; no brand logos or
         real products in scenes.
         Expressions that encourage effort: curious, determined, pleased. Never mocking,
         shaming or disappointed.

  Pip, the guide

         An original, friendly creature that is clearly not a real animal brand or known
         character. Pip must read as a helper, not a friend that needs the child; no romantic,
         needy or "don't leave me" poses.
         Recognisable at 48 px as an icon and at full screen.

  Math accuracy (non-negotiable)

         Any picture that shows quantities must show the exact count: a ten-rod has exactly
         10 units, a ten-frame has 10 cells, an array of 3 rows of 4 shows 12.
         Base-ten blocks keep correct proportions: a hundred-flat is 10 by 10 units, a ten-rod is
         10 units long.
         Decorative objects must not look countable near a problem, so children don't count
         the wrong things.
         Every counting illustration is checked by our team against the item before sign-off.

  Accessibility

         No meaning carried by color alone; regions differ by shape and landmark, not just hue.
         Text never baked into images; the app sets all words so they can be read aloud and
         translated.
         Busy backgrounds stay behind a calm area wherever a question appears.

  File formats for every item

         Layered master files (Procreate, Photoshop or Illustrator) and SVG where the item is
         vector.
         PNG exports at 1x, 2x and 3x; icon at the store sizes.
         Each character pose on a transparent background, no text in images.


  Contract terms to require
  These are the points GMIA's attorney should see in the agreement. They are not legal
  advice and the attorney should draft or approve the final contract.

  Ownership

         Full copyright assignment of all final art, characters and sketches to GMIA, not just
         a license. Pip is the brand; GMIA must own it outright.
         A "work made for hire" label alone is not enough: under US law it covers only certain
         kinds of commissioned work, and it has no effect for artists outside the US. Use an
         assignment, with work-for-hire as a backup.
         If the artist is in California, ask the attorney about work-for-hire wording, which can
         make the artist an employee under state law.
         For artists in Canada, the UK or the EU, a written waiver of moral rights, as far as local
         law allows.
         A license back for the artist to show the work in their portfolio after launch.

  Originality and AI

         Warranty that the work is original and doesn't copy any existing character, artwork or
         brand, with indemnity.

         No generative-AI imagery in any deliverable unless GMIA agrees in writing. The US
         Copyright Office does not register material generated by AI without human
         authorship, so AI art could leave Pip unprotected.
         Process files (sketches, layers) delivered as proof of authorship.

  Delivery and payment

         Milestones: sketches, color roughs, final. Payment split roughly 30% on signing, 40%
         on approved roughs, 30% on final delivery.
         Two rounds of revisions per milestone included; further rounds at an agreed hourly
         rate.
         Kill fee if GMIA cancels: commonly 25–50% of the unpaid fee depending on the stage
         reached.
         Confidentiality until launch; credit line in the app's About page and the store listing.

  Children's safety

         The artist receives no child data and doesn't take part in testing sessions unless the
         parents agree in writing.



  Budget and timeline
  These are planning estimates, not quotes. Agency artists with educational and character
  experience, buying full copyright, typically fall in these ranges; buying full ownership
  costs more than a license. Check the quotes against the Graphic Artists Guild Handbook:
  Pricing & Ethical Guidelines, 17th edition (MIT Press, 2025).
