  Summary
  Phase 2 turns the approved strategy into a buildable curriculum: 52 micro-skills for the
  MVP strand, a misconception library, the item and verification spec, the mastery engine
  rules, a placement test, and outline curricula for the lifelong map, Mental Math Strategies,
  Soroban, and Financial Intelligence. The MVP strand is specified to build-ready depth;
  later programs are specified to the level Phase 3 design needs.

  Carried forward from Phase 1 (approved)

         Beachhead: US families, ages 7–10, grades 2–4, plus homeschoolers.
         MVP strand: place value → addition and subtraction within 1,000 → multiplication
         meaning and facts to 10×10, in Number Kingdom.
         "Mental Math Strategies" with an honest Vedic history panel; soroban as a long-
         horizon track with a place-value mode in the MVP.
         No open AI chat for under-13s; deterministic math engine as the source of truth.
         No ads, no in-game purchases.

  Assumptions I made, which you can overrule

         Build path: the lean, founder-led path from Phase 1's budget, since the funded path
         was framed as waiting for pilot results.
         Piano Quest: still open. I designed the learner profile and parent report generically, so
         sharing an engine later stays possible without rework.
         Standards: aligned to Common Core codes, with a Georgia K–12 Mathematics
         Standards crosswalk column, since Georgia replaced Common Core-based standards
         in 2023–24 and is your likely pilot state.

  What Gate G2 needs (section 11): a paid math-education specialist reviews sections 1–5
  and signs off on the micro-skill map, misconceptions and item templates.

  Competency framework
  The MVP strand has 52 micro-skills in four domains; every micro-skill has a single ID, a
  standards code, named prerequisites and the representations its lessons use, and
  nothing unlocks until its prerequisites are Mastered.

          id , name , domain , ccss , ga_code , prerequisites[]

  Detection rules

          Each misconception is a function that, given an item, returns its predicted wrong
          answer. If the child's answer matches, the error is tagged.
          One match is logged as a possible misconception. Two matches across different items
          within five attempts confirm it and trigger remediation; this avoids reacting to slips.
          If no pattern matches, the error is tagged "unclassified" and the hint ladder runs.
          Unclassified errors that cluster in pilot data become candidates for new library
          entries.
          Remediation always ends by re-presenting an item like the one originally missed. The
          misconception is cleared only after three correct items of the diagnostic type.

  Formats required for the Mastered gate (at least three per skill): number entry; multiple
  choice with diagnostic distractors; build-it with a manipulative; true/false with
  explanation choice; and error analysis ("Pip got 34; what went wrong?").

  Verification pipeline (every template, every release)

     1. Exhaustive or sampled generation: enumerate all parameter combinations where
        feasible, otherwise 10,000 samples.
     2. Constraint check: every generated item satisfies its constraints; any that don't fail the
         build.
     3. Answer check: answer recomputed by an independent second implementation;
        mismatch fails the build.
     4. Distractor check: no distractor equals the correct answer; distractors are distinct.
     5. Step check: the final step of the worked example equals the answer.
     6. Text check: reading level within the band; no unsafe or culturally loaded words in
         contexts.

  Mastery engine specification
  A micro-skill becomes Mastered only when two independent checks agree: the
  knowledge-tracing estimate reaches 0.95 and the observable gate from Phase 1 is met;
  that pairing prevents both lucky streaks and a model that is too optimistic.

  States and transitions


     From                              To           Trigger

     Locked                            Learning     All prerequisites Mastered (or placed out)

     Learning                          Practiced    Lesson cycle complete through guided practice

     Practiced                         Mastered     P(known) ≥ 0.95 and ≥90% on ≥10 independent
                                                    items, ≥3 formats, 1 transfer and 1 explain item
                                                    correct

     Mastered                          Certified    7-day and 30-day checks each ≥80%

     Mastered or Certified             Practiced    A missed retention check, or two confirmed
                                                    misconceptions in review

     Any                               Placed-out   Placement test shows mastery; still receives a 7-
                                                    day check


  Knowledge tracing (Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, per micro-skill)

         Four parameters: prior known (L0), learn rate (T), guess (G), slip (S); a 0.95 threshold
         is the common operational choice (MetricGate).
         Starting values until pilot data exist: L0 = 0.10 (or set by placement), T = 0.15, S = 0.10,
         G by format: number entry 0.05, 4-option multiple choice 0.25, true/false 0.50.
         Only independent items update the estimate; hinted items at level 2 or above are
         logged but don't count.
         Refit parameters per skill once a skill has 200+ learners' data; flag skills whose fitted
         learn rate stays near zero, since that usually means the skill mixes two skills.

  Session composition (default; tuned in pilot)

         About 40% new learning, 30% practice on current skills, 30% interleaved review of
         mastered skills. Interleaving starts only after a skill has been taught in a blocked
         lesson, as the research recommends.
         Session length: age 7, 15 minutes default and 20 maximum; ages 8–10, 20 default and
         30 maximum. A natural stopping point always ends the session; there are no streak
         penalties.

  Review scheduler

         Retention checks at 7, 30 and 90 days after Mastered, each 4–5 interleaved items,
         delivered inside normal sessions rather than as tests.
         Between checks, mastered skills reappear in review at expanding gaps (2, 4, 10, 20
         days).
         If a learner is away, overdue checks are spread out, at most two checks per session.

  Next-activity rule (in order)

     1. Any confirmed misconception → remediation.
     2. Any due retention check → include it.
     3. Current Learning or Practiced skill with the lowest P(known).
     4. Otherwise unlock the next skill whose prerequisites are met, preferring the one on
        the shortest path to the strand goal.

  Placement assessment design
  Placement is an adaptive walk down the prerequisite graph that finishes in 15 minutes or
  less, places the child on the graph rather than in a grade, and errs toward placing slightly
  too low, because a too-easy start costs a few minutes while a too-hard start costs
  confidence.

  Design

         Presented as "Explore the Kingdom map," not a test; no score shown to the child.
         Starting point set by the caregiver's answer to "Which math is your child working on
         now?" (four plain-language options), not by age alone.
         8 anchor skills probed first, each with 2–3 items: PV04, PV10, AS05, AS07, AS12, AS15,
         MU03, MU12.
         Rule per anchor: 3 of 3 correct → mark it and its ancestors Placed-out, probe further
         along; 0–1 of 3 → probe its prerequisites; 2 of 3 → one more item decides.
         Misconception matches during placement are recorded and seed the first week's
         remediation.
         Hard stop at 25 items or 15 minutes; untested skills default to Locked or Learning.

  Output to the parent (on screen and in the first report)

         "Already strong at," "Ready to learn next," and "We'll strengthen first," each in plain
         words with one example item.
         The first two weeks' plan.

  Validation in the pilot (Phase 1 acceptance criterion): retest 20+ children within 7 days;
  placements must agree on at least 80% of micro-skills. Also compare placement with an
  independent measure, a short teacher- or parent-administered paper probe, for 30
  children.

  Mental Math Strategies curriculum
  Ten techniques are sequenced by prerequisite, five of them in the MVP; each is taught
  with the algebra or place-value reason behind it, compared side by side with the standard
  method, and assessed on accuracy, explanation and choosing when to use it, never on
  speed alone.

  History panel (shown to learners and parents, plain language). Several techniques come
  from Vedic Mathematics, a book by Bharati Krishna Tirtha published in 1965, after his
  death. He presented 16 short Sanskrit phrases (sutras) as rules for calculation. Historians,
  including the mathematician S. G. Dani, and the book's own editor note the sutras are not
  found in known Vedic texts. GMIA teaches these methods because the math is sound and
  sometimes useful, and celebrates the documented history of Indian mathematics
  separately (for example, place-value numerals with zero and the Śulba-sūtras' geometry).

  Lesson pattern per technique

     1. Puzzle hook: "Can you find 1,000 − 368 without borrowing?"
     2. Visual demonstration with blocks, soroban or an area model.
     3. The reason, at the child's level (T3: "999 never needs borrowing").
     4. Side-by-side with the standard method on the same problem.
     5. Guided, then independent practice.
     6. "Which way would you choose?" items mixing problems where the technique helps
        and where it doesn't.
     7. Retention checks at 7, 30 and 90 days, like any other skill.

  Mastery for a technique: ≥90% accuracy on 10 independent items, 1 correct "why it
  works" item, and ≥4 of 5 correct method-choice items. Speed is reported to parents only
  after accuracy is met.


  Soroban and mental abacus curriculum
  The Soroban Academy is an optional, multi-year track of eight levels; the MVP ships Levels
  1–2 as a place-value tool inside the core lessons, and every level checks that the child can
  say what the beads mean, not only move them fast.

  Understanding checks at every level

         Translation items: soroban → numeral → base-ten blocks, both directions.
         "Why" items: "Why did you push the 5-bead down and take 2 away?"
         Transfer: the same problem on paper with a strategy from section 7.

  Honest positioning. The 3-year RCT found arithmetic gains after about 3 hours a week of
  training; the 1-year US trial found no computation advantage, especially for younger first
  graders. Parents are told the track rewards long, steady practice, and GMIA makes no
  claims about memory or intelligence. Soroban practice counts toward the same
  competencies, so time spent here is never wasted for the core curriculum.

  Human review: an experienced soroban instructor reviews Levels 4–8 move sequences
  and benchmarks before Phase 5.


  Financial intelligence and entrepreneurship curriculum
  The financial strand follows the six topics of the 2021 National Standards for Personal
  Financial Education (Council for Economic Education and Jump$tart): earning income,
  spending, saving, investing, managing credit and managing risk. Every activity is first a
  math lesson with a measurable math objective, and it uses play money only.

  Worked example for Level 1: lemons $0.20 + sugar $0.05 + cup $0.05 = $0.30 per cup.

  Simulation rules

          Demand is a published table (price × weather → customers), so every result can be
          checked by hand; no hidden randomness at Levels 1–2.
          Losses happen and are explained, never punished: the learner keeps playing and can
          see what would have changed the result.
          No real money, no links to real financial products, no advice. Investing content is
          labeled "how it works," not "what to buy."
          Business scenarios later expand to the directive's list (bakery, grocery, toy company,
          real estate), each reusing the same profit and inventory engine.


  Real-world application library
  Every micro-skill needs at least one "Apply" scenario in an unfamiliar context; the library
  starts with 20 scenarios and grows in Phase 3 until every one of the 52 MVP skills has at
  least one, and each scenario is a story wrapper around engine-generated numbers, so one
  scenario yields thousands of distinct items.

  Scenario schema: id , context , skills[] , story_template , param_bindings (which
  item parameters fill the story), visual , reading_level , cultural_review (pass/fail
  with reviewer), objective (one sentence a parent can read).
