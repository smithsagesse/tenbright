  Summary
  Recommendation: parent-only accounts, children as profiles under them, "email plus" or
  "text plus" parental consent at launch, and an event-log sync to a managed Postgres
  backend. Each choice rests on one fact: Tenbright never discloses children's data to third
  parties, which the COPPA Rule makes the condition for the lighter consent methods.

  This is a plan for the privacy attorney to review, not legal advice; section 6 lists the
  questions that must be answered before any child data leaves a device.

  Decisions this plan needs

         Consent method at launch: email plus or text plus (recommended), or a stronger
         method through a free verification service.
         Backend: managed Postgres with row-level security (recommended), or a document
         database.
         Whether to join an FTC-approved COPPA Safe Harbor program before or after the
         pilot.
         The retention periods proposed in section 4.
         Engage the privacy attorney (Phase 1 budget: about $5–15k) to review this plan, the
         privacy notice and the consent flow.


  What data moves off the device

  the parent's weekly report leaves the device; nothing goes to advertisers, analytics

  Two rules that keep this small

         The child side never sends free text. Every child input is a number, a choice or a bead
         position, so there is nothing personal to leak.
         The AI phrasing service, when it arrives, receives only engine facts such as "38 + 27,
         the ones make 15", never a child identifier, so it should not count as a disclosure of a
         child's personal information. This needs the attorney's confirmation (section 6).


  Verifiable parental consent
  The COPPA Rule lists nine consent methods (16 CFR 312.5); the two lightest, "email plus"
  and "text plus," are available only to operators that do not disclose children's personal
  information, which is Tenbright's design, so they are the recommended launch method.

  Recommended flow

     1. Parent creates an account with email and password; no child data yet.
     2. Tenbright shows the direct notice (what we collect, why, retention, rights) and asks
        for consent.
     3. Email plus: a consent link goes to the parent's email; after consent, a confirmation
        email follows, with a link to revoke at any time.
     4. Only after step 3 completes can the parent add a child profile.
     5. If a parent later starts a paid plan, the card transaction can serve as a second,
        stronger record of consent.

  If we ever add a feature that discloses child data (say, sharing a report with a teacher's
  account), email plus stops being enough for that feature and we would need a stronger
  method and a separate consent, per the amended Rule.


  Backend and sync architecture
  Recommendation: keep the device as the place learning happens and sync an append-
  only log of answer events to a managed Postgres database. Each device rebuilds mastery
  from the same events, so two devices never disagree for long and nothing is lost offline.

  How sync works

         Every answer becomes an event with a random ID, the child profile ID, the skill, the
         result and a timestamp; events are only ever added, never edited.
         The engine replays events to compute mastery, so any device with the events gets the
         same state.
         Offline answers queue on the device and upload when the connection returns;
         duplicate event IDs are ignored.
         Deleting a child deletes all their events and derived state on the server, then on every
         device at next sync.

  Access rules: a parent can read and write only their own family's rows; no child login
  exists; staff access to production data is limited to named people, logged, and used only
  for support with the parent's permission.

  Hosting region: US only at launch, matching the beachhead; revisit for GDPR when
  expanding (Phase 6).


  Security program and retention schedule
  The amended COPPA Rule, in force since April 22, 2026, requires a written information
  security program and a published retention policy with deletion timeframes; both are
  drafted here as proposals for the attorney.

  Written security program (outline)

         Named coordinator: you, as founder, until a staff member takes it over.
         Annual written risk assessment of child data, plus one before launch.
         Safeguards: encryption in transit and at rest, row-level access rules, multi-factor sign-
         in for every staff account, no production data on personal laptops, logged staff
         access.
         Vendor assurances: a data-processing agreement with each processor (hosting,
         email, SMS, payments) confirming they protect the data and use it only for our
         service.
         Testing: dependency and vulnerability scanning in the build, and an outside security
         review before public launch.
         Incident response: who decides, how parents are told, and the timelines state breach
         laws require.
         Yearly review of the program itself.

  Questions for the attorney, cost and timeline
  The build can start the day the attorney answers the first three questions; the rest can be
  settled during the build.

  Questions

     1. Does our design (no third-party disclosure; processors only) qualify us for email plus
        or text plus under 312.5(b)(2)(viii)–(ix)?
     2. Is sending engine-only facts (no child identifier) to an AI wording service a
        "disclosure" of a child's personal information?
     3. Is our direct notice and online privacy notice complete under the amended Rule? (We
         will draft both.)
     4. Are the retention periods in section 4 defensible, especially 13 months for raw answer
        events?
     5. Which state design codes apply to a family-subscription education product, and what
        assessments do they require?
     6. Is a Safe Harbor membership worth it before the pilot, or only before public launch?
     7. Can the pilot run on the current on-device preview, with parents' signed consent
         forms, while the backend is built?
     8. What should our terms say about a parent's right to review and delete, and about the
        subscription (clear cancellation)?
